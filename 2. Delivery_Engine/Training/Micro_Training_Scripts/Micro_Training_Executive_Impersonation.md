# Micro-Training Module
## Topic: Executive Impersonation — Don’t Trust Urgent Requests
NorthStar Security — Human-Layer Training
Duration: 3–4 minutes

---

## 1. What Happened
You clicked on a simulated phishing email designed to look like it came from an executive.

This is one of the most common real-world attack types targeting SMBs.

---

## 2. How Executive Impersonation Works
Attackers pretend to be:
- CEO
- CFO
- HR
- IT
- Finance

They often use:
- Urgent language
- Quick requests
- Short messages
- “Can you do this for me quickly?”

---

## 3. Red Flags You Missed
- The sender’s email didn’t match the real executive
- The request was vague
- The link pointed to an unfamiliar domain
- The message created urgency without context

---

## 4. How To Verify Executive Requests
### Step 1 — Check the sender address
Executives rarely email from personal or unfamiliar accounts.

### Step 2 — Slow down
Urgency is a tactic.

### Step 3 — Confirm through another channel
- Teams/Slack
- Phone
- In-person
- Known internal email

### Step 4 — Never click links in unexpected executive emails

---

## 5. What To Do Next Time
- Verify before acting
- Don’t respond to suspicious executive emails
- Report the message
- Trust your instincts — if it feels off, it probably is

---

## 6. Final Reminder
Attackers rely on authority and urgency.
Your awareness protects both you and the organization.
