# NorthStar + SwarmCommand — Revenue Map

**Purpose:** The detailed plan for funding the multi-year arc described in `VISION.md`. Three revenue lanes that run in parallel. Specific to Matt's situation (Kelowna BC, in an employment course, wage-subsidy eligible, self-taught, 9 months in).

**Operating principle:** **Selling is discovery, not pitching.** You don't sell NorthStar yet. You learn what people need so NorthStar can become something they'll pay for. This removes ~80% of the fear that "I don't know how to sell."

**Update rule:** Weekly review every Friday. Add what you learned. Adjust tactics, not strategy.

**Last reviewed:** 2026-05-23

## The Three Lanes

| Lane | Purpose | Timeline | Funded by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lane 1: Survival Income** | Pay the bills via local employment | Now → 12 months | Wage + wage subsidy (employer pays ~50%, program covers ~50%) |
| **Lane 2: Freelance Income** | Prove the engineering, build case studies | 30 → 180 days | Contra + Upwork (see `THIRTY_DAY_PLAN.md`) |
| **Lane 3: NorthStar Revenue** | The long game — Stage A → B → C | 12 → 36 months | MSP discovery → pilots → retainers |

All three run **at the same time.** Lane 1 keeps you alive. Lane 2 proves the work. Lane 3 builds the company. Treating them sequentially is what kills solo founders.

## LANE 1 — Survival Income (Kelowna BC)

### Why this matters

You're in an employment course with a wage subsidy. **The subsidy is leverage, not a disadvantage.** Many employers actively prefer subsidized hires because their effective cost is cut in half. Use this.

### What program you're likely in (confirm with your case manager)

The most common BC programs that match your description ("income grant pays half my wage"):
- **WorkBC Wage Subsidy Program** (most common — covers up to 50% of wages for ~24 weeks)
- **Canada–British Columbia Job Grant** (training-focused)
- **Employment Program of BC (EPBC)** wage subsidy stream

**Action:** Ask your case manager *exactly* what program covers your wage, the **maximum weekly subsidy amount**, the **eligible weeks**, and whether **MSP / IT security employers qualify**. Write the answer in your notes. You'll repeat this in every interview.

### Job categories realistic for you right now

You are 9 months self-taught with a working LangGraph + LLM scoring system in production. That puts you above "absolute beginner" and below "intermediate engineer." Aim at these:

| Role | Why it's a fit | Stretch level |
|---|---|---|
| **IT Helpdesk / Tier 1 Support** | Foundational. Every IT career starts here. Pays bills. | Easy entry |
| **MSP Technician (Junior)** | Direct exposure to the buyer market you're building for. Doubles as research. | Easy entry |
| **Junior SOC Analyst / Security Ops Tier 1** | Aligns with NorthStar. Shows you're serious about cybersecurity. | Medium |
| **Junior Python Developer** | You can code. Show the LangGraph repo. | Medium |
| **IT Coordinator / Sysadmin Junior** | Mixed IT + admin work. Pays decently. | Medium |
| **QA / Test Analyst** | Eval-harness experience translates directly. | Medium |
| **Cybersecurity Internship / Co-op** | Local college / UBCO often has these. | Easy entry, possibly lower-paid |

**Avoid:** "Senior Engineer," "Architect," "Lead." Don't waste time applying. The wage subsidy + 9-months-experience math doesn't work for those roles.

### How to leverage the wage subsidy in interviews

**Don't lead with it.** Lead with what you can do (LangGraph scoring, eval harness, 472 tests, working demo). Once they're interested, then introduce the subsidy as a *de-risker*, not a justification.

**Script for the moment they ask "tell me about yourself":**

> "I'm a self-taught engineer 9 months in. Built a production AI email-risk scoring system in Python with LangGraph, full eval harness, 472 tests green. I'm in the WorkBC employment program right now, which means an employer hiring me has about half of my wage covered by the program for the first [X] weeks — so the net cost to your business is significantly lower than a typical junior hire. Happy to put you in touch with my case manager if that helps your hiring conversation."

**Why this works:**
- Leads with capability (the LangGraph thing is real and impressive).
- Names the gap honestly (self-taught, 9 months).
- Reframes the subsidy as a benefit *to them*.
- Offers next-step proof (case manager contact).

## LANE 2 — Freelance Income

See `THIRTY_DAY_PLAN.md` — already detailed. Two additions:

### Reframe: discovery before pitch on freelance platforms too

When you write a Contra / Upwork proposal, **do not lead with what you'll build.** Lead with one question that proves you understood the brief, *then* the offer. This 3x's reply rates.

Bad proposal opening: *"I can build your LangGraph workflow with retry and JSON validation in 5 days for $500."*

Good proposal opening: *"Before I quote, one question: when the model returns malformed JSON today, does your system fail open (pass it through) or fail closed (reject)? My approach is different depending on the answer."*

### Service offer tightening

Per `THIRTY_DAY_PLAN.md`, you have three offers:
1. LangGraph workflow with validated JSON output
2. AI evaluation harness for existing LLM app
3. xAI / Claude / OpenAI integration cleanup

Add a **fourth wedge offer** for higher-fee work that converts Lane 2 into Lane 3 over time:

4. **"AI eval gate audit"** — flat $500. You run their existing prompt or workflow against your 40-case eval pattern (adapted to their domain). Deliverable: pass/fail report with per-failure-mode analysis. Takes 2–4 hours of your time. Positions you as the auditable-AI expert. Lands you cases that often turn into NorthStar discovery conversations.

## LANE 3 — NorthStar Revenue (The 90-Day Discovery Program)

**This lane has zero revenue today.** The goal for the next 90 days is **not revenue — it is 30 discovery conversations** with MSP owners and small-business security buyers. The revenue comes from what you learn.

### Why discovery, not pitching

You cannot sell what you don't yet understand the buyer needs. Discovery conversations:
- Cost you nothing.
- Teach you the exact language buyers use.
- Build a pipeline of warm contacts who already trust you.
- Surface the 1–2 buyers who become free pilots, which become paid contracts.

### The discovery program structure

**Goal:** 30 MSP / SMB security discovery conversations in 90 days. **10 per month.**

**Format:** 15 minutes. Phone, video, or in-person. **Not selling.** Explicitly framed as "I'm researching the market, not pitching."

### Who to talk to

| Target | How to find them | How to reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Local Kelowna MSPs** | Google "managed service provider Kelowna" + Chamber directory | Cold call OR LinkedIn DM the owner |
| **BC Interior MSPs** | Same search radius extended to Vernon, Penticton, Kamloops, Salmon Arm | LinkedIn DM |
| **Greater Vancouver MSPs** | LinkedIn search "Owner" + "MSP" + Vancouver | LinkedIn DM |
| **MSP-adjacent IT consultants** | LinkedIn search "IT consultant" + Okanagan | LinkedIn DM |
| **Local SMBs that recently had a breach** | Google News alerts: "BC small business breach", "Okanagan ransomware" | Polite email referencing the public news |
| **r/msp lurkers** | Reddit. **Do NOT spam.** Comment thoughtfully on threads, build presence, take warm DMs. | Reddit, after 2-3 weeks of presence |
| **Insurance brokers handling cyber policies** | Local Kelowna commercial insurance brokers | Cold call: "I'm researching cyber risk for SMBs" |

### The discovery call script (15 minutes)

**Opening (30 seconds):**
> "Hi, this is Matt. I'm building security tooling for MSPs out of Kelowna. I'm in a research phase, not a sales phase. Could I have 15 minutes to ask you five questions about how your shop handles client email security incidents? I'm not pitching anything — I just want to learn."

**The five questions (10 minutes — let them talk, take notes):**

1. **Volume:** "Roughly what percentage of your support tickets in the last 6 months were security-related?"
2. **Playbook:** "When a client reports a phishing email, what's your standard response — minute by minute?"
3. **Proof:** "If a client asks 'how do you know you caught it?', what do you show them?"
4. **Adoption trigger:** "What would have to be true for you to add a new email-security tool to your stack?"
5. **Network:** "Who else in your network should I be talking to?"

**Close (2 minutes):**
> "This was incredibly helpful. May I send you a one-page summary of what I learned across all my discovery calls when I'm done? Some MSPs find that useful even if we never work together. And — if you're open to it — I'll occasionally check in over the next few months."

**After:** Send a thank-you email within 24 hours. Add them to a simple spreadsheet (date, name, MSP, key quote, next action).

### Why this works

- You're not selling. They relax.
- You're asking them to talk about themselves. They like it.
- Question 4 is the gold one. It tells you *exactly* what your product has to look like.
- Question 5 multiplies your network for free.
- The follow-up cadence (1 paragraph every 2-3 months) keeps you top-of-mind without pestering.

### Pricing experiments (when discovery turns into a pilot offer)

Once you've done 10+ discovery calls and one MSP says "actually, this sounds useful," you propose a pilot. Test these price points:

| Tier | Format | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free pilot #1** | 1 MSP, 1 tenant, 30 days, full eval report + override CLI access | $0 | Your case study. Their proof. Both win. |
| **Paid pilot #2 onwards** | Same scope | $500–$1,000 one-time | Real money. Proves people will pay. |
| **Per-tenant subscription** | $50–$150 / mo per SMB tenant | recurring | Standard MSP-resold pattern. |
| **MSP retainer (flat)** | $500–$2,000 / mo for unlimited tenants in their book | recurring | What MSPs actually want — predictable. |
| **Eval gate audit (one-off)** | $500 flat | one-off | Lower-friction entry point. Often converts to retainer. |

**Don't pick one yet.** Use the first 3 pilots to test 2–3 price points and see what bites.

## What Not To Do

These are the failure modes of solo technical founders. Avoid them.

1. **Don't build new features instead of doing outreach.** Building feels productive. It's not, this quarter.
2. **Don't pitch on the first discovery call.** Pitching kills future referrals.
3. **Don't lower your wage subsidy ask.** The program covers what it covers. Stand on it.
4. **Don't fake authority.** If an MSP asks something you don't know, say "I don't know — I'll find out and email you." Trust > confidence.
5. **Don't quote prices before pilot #2.** First pilot free, learn fast, then charge.
6. **Don't try to compete with CrowdStrike on signature counts.** Wrong axis. (See `VISION.md`.)
7. **Don't pay for premium platforms (Upwork Plus, LinkedIn Premium, paid threat feeds) yet.** None of them earn you revenue at this stage.
8. **Don't start a second product.** Park ideas in `think_sheet.md`. Period.
