# Todd Tuesday MSP Call Anchor Card

**Purpose:** Phone-openable anchor for Matt's first MSP conversation with Todd. This is not a pitch deck, not a close script, and not a buyer-facing claim sheet. It is a calm one-page guide so the conversation has structure without overbuilding.

**Meeting posture:** learning / relationship-opening call. Success means Todd explains how his MSP sees client email-fraud risk, what evidence would matter, and whether there is a sensible next conversation.

**Not selling:** Tuesday is not a sales call. The job is to learn whether Todd sees the same problem in the field, understand what evidence would matter to an MSP, and open enough trust for a useful second conversation.

---

## Opening Anchor

I am building this because small businesses get hurt by real-looking emails from trusted vendors, executives, or finance contacts where the payment details, behavior, timing, or verification path quietly changed.

The goal is not to replace the MSP or make unsupported security claims. The goal is to give the MSP a clearer evidence record: why something looked risky, what was observed, what still needs verification, and what can be shown back to a client during review.

For today, I mostly want to learn whether this matches a real pain you see with SMB clients.

---

## One-Sentence Version

I am building an email-fraud decision-support and evidence layer for MSPs, focused on vendor-payment fraud, executive impersonation, phishing, and audit-ready review notes for suspicious emails.

---

## Questions To Keep The Conversation Moving

1. When one of your SMB clients asks, "Is this email real?", how does that usually get handled today?

2. Which email-fraud problems create the most stress for your clients: vendor-payment changes, executive impersonation, credential phishing, ransomware precursor signs, or something else?

3. When a client or insurer asks what was done about a suspicious email, what evidence do you currently have to show them?

4. What would make an MSP trust a tool like this enough to test it with one low-risk client or synthetic sample set?

5. If this is not the right shape yet, what is the missing piece that would make it worth a second conversation?

---

## If It Goes Quiet

- "The part I am trying to understand is whether MSPs need better evidence around these calls, or whether detection alone is already covered well enough."
- "I am not trying to sell you a finished platform today. I am trying to find out whether this direction solves a problem MSPs actually feel."
- "If you were advising me not to waste time, what would you tell me to prove first?"

---

## What To Avoid Saying

- Do not say it blocks, quarantines, remediates, or replaces Microsoft / Google security today.
- Do not say it makes anyone compliant, certified, approved, or guaranteed safer.
- Do not promise insurance outcomes, premium impact, or claim approval.
- Do not over-explain the 70-agent swarm unless Todd asks about the long-term architecture.

---

## Simple Close

If this lines up with what you see, the next useful step is not a sale. It is a second conversation where I show a small phone-openable evidence sample and ask what would have to be true for an MSP to test this safely.

If it does not line up, the useful outcome is knowing that now and hearing what I should correct.
