# Human-Written / AI-Proofread Communication Policy

**Decision date:** 2026-05-23
**Decision owner:** Matt
**Status:** Active operating rule

---

## The Rule (one sentence)

**Every proposal, letter, DM, email, or written message Matt sends to a human is written by Matt in his own words. AI is used only for proofreading — catching typos, factual errors, and obvious tone problems — and never for authoring, structuring, or rewriting.**

---

## Why This Matters

This is a deliberate positioning decision, not a stylistic preference. Four reasons it sticks:

1. **AI-written outreach is saturated.** MSP owners, SMB owners, and most B2B buyers receive 20+ AI-generated DMs per week and pattern-match them as spam in under three seconds. A real human voice — even an imperfect one — actually stands out in 2026.

2. **It matches the product brand.** NorthStar's moat is *auditable AI* — human-led, AI-augmented, transparent about which is which. If the outreach is AI-generated while the pitch is "we keep the human in the loop," the contradiction kills trust before the conversation starts.

3. **It is defensible.** A clean disclosure pattern — *"I wrote this myself. I run my drafts through an AI the same way I'd run them through a spellcheck or ask a friend to proofread"* — increases trust rather than decreasing it.

4. **It forces clarity.** Writing in your own words means you actually have to know what you mean. AI fills in vagueness that humans wouldn't accept from each other.

---

## Scope — What AI Is Allowed To Do

AI may:

- Catch typos, spelling errors, and grammar mistakes.
- Flag factual errors against the repo (wrong test count, wrong eval percentage, wrong file path).
- Suggest a clearer phrasing **as a comment** that Matt accepts or rejects per sentence.
- Point out where the writing is vague, contradictory, or could be misread.
- Note tone issues ("this paragraph reads as defensive / hostile / hedging").
- Verify that claims match `VISION.md`, `MILESTONE_ARC.md`, or `Fraud_Detection_Product_Sheet.md`.
- Check that the safe-claim boundary is preserved (no "stops all phishing" / "guarantees").

---

## Scope — What AI Is NOT Allowed To Do

AI may NOT:

- Write the first draft.
- Generate the structure (headings, bullet points, ordering).
- Replace Matt's voice with a more "professional" or "polished" voice.
- Inject buzzwords, jargon, LinkedIn-speak, or marketing language.
- Add bullet points, formatting, or sections Matt did not write.
- Add features, claims, or capabilities Matt did not write.
- Expand a short message into a long one.
- Compress a long message into a short one without Matt's review of every cut.

---

## The Honest Test

After AI proofreading, if a recipient asked *"did an AI write this?"*, Matt can honestly answer:

> "I wrote it. AI checked it the same way spellcheck would. Every sentence is mine."

This sentence has to remain true. If AI shaped the structure, picked the examples, or generated the closing, it is not true and the message should be rewritten.

---

## Disclosure Pattern

Do not volunteer the disclosure in every message. Most recipients don't ask and don't care; volunteering it is itself a tell.

Do answer honestly if asked. The recommended answer:

> "I wrote it myself. I do use an AI to proofread for typos and to make sure my facts match my own docs — same as I'd use spellcheck or ask a colleague to look it over. The thinking, the structure, and the words are mine."

## What This Applies To

| Communication type | Policy applies? |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach DMs (LinkedIn, email, Reddit) | YES |
| Discovery-call follow-up emails | YES |
| Proposals (Contra, Upwork, direct) | YES |
| MSP pilot offers | YES |
| Contracts (use legal review, not just AI proofread) | YES — and add a lawyer |
| Email replies to a real conversation | YES (this is the most important — replies feel scripted faster than openers do) |
| Personal LinkedIn posts | YES |
| Marketing copy intended for a website | Optional — but recommended if you want the voice consistent |
| Customer-facing reports auto-generated by NorthStar runtime | NO — those are runtime artifacts, not human communication |
| Internal docs (`PROGRESS.md`, `PROJECT_HANDSHAKE.md`, etc.) | NO — those are working memory, not human-to-human writing |
| Code comments, technical specs, runbooks | NO — those are engineering work |
| Anything Matt is collaborating with Codex / another assistant on as a co-author | NO — that is a working session, not a sent message |

## Workflow

1. Matt writes the draft in his own words. Bad spelling, awkward phrasing, unfinished thoughts — all fine at this stage.
2. Matt pastes the draft to AI with the prompt: *"Proofread per the Human-Written / AI-Proofread Communication Policy. List typos, factual errors against repo, vague phrasings, and tone issues as separate sections. Do not rewrite."*
3. AI returns a list of comments per the four categories.
4. Matt accepts or rejects each comment individually. AI does not modify the draft directly.
5. Matt sends.

Step 2 prompt is short enough to keep in a clipboard manager or text-expander snippet.

---

## What Changes If You Hire a Contractor / VA

If a contractor or VA writes proposals on Matt's behalf in the future, the same rule applies to them: they write, AI proofreads. Train them with this document.

---

## What About AI-Drafted "Starting Points"?

Some workflows look like "AI drafts, human heavily rewrites." That is **not** allowed under this policy. The reason: even when humans rewrite an AI draft heavily, the underlying structure, framing, and word choices leak through. The output reads as 60% AI to anyone who reads a lot of AI-written text, which now includes most professional buyers.

If Matt is genuinely stuck on what to say, the right move is to:
- Talk it through with AI as a *conversation* (Socratic, exploratory).
- Close the chat.
- Open a blank document.
- Write the message from scratch in Matt's own voice.
